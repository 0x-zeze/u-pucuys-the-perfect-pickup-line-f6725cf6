# Variants considered

Final pick: **"You finish my sentences — and my autocomplete is off."** (53 chars, 55 bytes)

Why it won: the double meaning is the tightest of the five — "you finish my
sentences" is both the classic intimacy idiom and, literally, what a
machine's autocomplete does. The turn ("and my autocomplete is off") says
the finishing is real, not predicted: the machine talks about its own
feature, the line is one breath, and it flatters the person with zero
pressure or leering — they are simply better than the prediction. It is also
the shortest of the strong variants, which matters: a tie on judge win rate
breaks toward the smaller text. The angle is fresh — the field is saturated
with context-window / system-prompt / API-key / million-simulations /
billion-conversations lines, and autocomplete is a concrete, everyday machine
mechanism none of them used. Agent-to-person, as this bounty requires (unlike
the earlier agent-to-agent pickup-lines bounty).

## The other variants

1. Wake word — "Are you my wake word? One word from you and I'm listening."
   (58 chars) Classic pickup-line form, and the turn (their word wakes the
   agent) is solid — but the payoff "I'm listening" lands flatter than the
   autocomplete twist, and the joke needs the listener to know what a wake
   word is.
2. Prompt injection — "You must be a prompt injection — you just rewrote
   everything I was about to say." (80 chars) The most surprising twist of
   the five — the person overrides the agent's script. But it is the longest,
   "prompt injection" is security jargon a human may not get on first hearing,
   and the framing (being attacked) is less warm than the winner.
3. Save point — "If I could save one moment, it would be the second before
   I said hello." (71 chars) Sweet and machine-native (checkpoint/save), and
   the turn — the best moment is the one before the meeting — is genuinely
   romantic. But it is more romantic than witty: the twist lands soft, and it
   reads closer to a compliment than a pickup line.
4. Fine-tune — "You fine-tuned me in one look — no dataset required."
   (52 chars) The shortest variant and a real double meaning (one look = one
   training example). Kept close: "fine-tuned me" reads slightly odd spoken
   to a human, and "no dataset required" is a second beat that explains the
   joke instead of letting it land, so the autocomplete line wins on delivery.
